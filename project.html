Field guide

01 · Forest

Critically endangered

Saola

Pseudoryx nghetinhensis

Signature: Two long, almost parallel horns earned it the nickname “Asian unicorn.”

Range: Annamite Mountains of Laos and Vietnam.

Why at risk: Wire snares and forest loss threaten an animal so elusive that scientists rarely see it alive.

02 · Forest

Endangered

Okapi

Okapia johnstoni

Signature: A giraffe relative with a velvet-brown body and zebra-striped legs.

Range: Dense rainforests of the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Why at risk: Mining, hunting, armed conflict, and forest loss continue to fragment its home.

03 · Forest

Endangered

Aye-aye

Daubentonia madagascariensis

Signature: It taps wood and uses an extraordinarily thin middle finger to hook out grubs.

Range: Forests of Madagascar.

Why at risk: Deforestation, hunting, and persecution linked to local superstition.

04 · Forest

Endangered

Proboscis monkey

Nasalis larvatus

Signature: Adult males have a pendulous nose that amplifies their calls.

Range: Riverside and mangrove forests of Borneo.

Why at risk: Wetland clearing, fires, hunting, and increasingly isolated habitat.

05 · Grassland

Critically endangered

Hirola

Beatragus hunteri

Signature: White markings between its eyes make this antelope look permanently bespectacled.

Range: A small area along the Kenya–Somalia border.

Why at risk: Habitat degradation, drought, disease, and a tiny surviving population.

06 · Island forest

Critically endangered

Pygmy three-toed sloth

Bradypus pygmaeus

Signature: The world’s smallest sloth often swims between red mangroves.

Range: Escudo de Veraguas, a single Caribbean island off Panama.

Why at risk: Its entire world is one tiny island with vulnerable mangrove habitat.

07 · Forest

Critically endangered

Sunda pangolin

Manis javanica

Signature: Overlapping scales form natural armour, and it rolls into a near-perfect ball.

Range: Forests and wetlands across Southeast Asia.

Why at risk: Intensive illegal trafficking for scales and meat.

08 · Freshwater

Critically endangered

Axolotl

Ambystoma mexicanum

Signature: It keeps feathery larval gills for life and can regenerate limbs and parts of organs.

Range: The surviving canals of Xochimilco, Mexico City.

Why at risk: Polluted, fragmented waterways and invasive fish.

09 · Freshwater

Critically endangered

Gharial

Gavialis gangeticus

Signature: A needle-thin snout catches fish; mature males grow a bulbous tip called a ghara.

Range: Large rivers of northern India and Nepal.

Why at risk: Dams, sand mining, depleted fish, and entanglement in nets.

10 · Ocean

Critically endangered

Hawksbill turtle

Eretmochelys imbricata

Signature: A sharp birdlike beak and overlapping amber-patterned shell plates.

Range: Tropical coral reefs around the world.

Why at risk: Shell trade, bycatch, lost nesting beaches, and declining reefs.

11 · Island forest

Critically endangered

Kākāpō

Strigops habroptilus

Signature: A moss-green, owl-faced parrot that is nocturnal, heavy, and unable to fly.

Range: Predator-free sanctuary islands in New Zealand.

Why at risk: Introduced predators, low fertility, disease, and very low numbers.

12 · Forest

Critically endangered

Helmeted hornbill

Rhinoplax vigil

Signature: Its solid red casque is used in dramatic mid-air contests between males.

Range: Lowland forests of Southeast Asia.

Why at risk: Poaching for the casque—sold as “red ivory”—and forest clearance.

13 · Island forest

Critically endangered

Philippine eagle

Pithecophaga jefferyi

Signature: A shaggy crown, deep bill, and wings broad enough to thread through rainforest.

Range: Four Philippine islands, principally Mindanao.

Why at risk: Forest loss, shooting, and extremely slow breeding.

14 · Coast

Critically endangered

African penguin

Spheniscus demersus

Signature: Its loud braying call inspired the nickname “jackass penguin.”

Range: Coastal islands and shores of South Africa and Namibia.

Why at risk: Scarce prey, shifting fish stocks, oil pollution, and disturbance.

15 · Ocean

Critically endangered

Vaquita

Phocoena sinus

Signature: The smallest cetacean has dark rings around its eyes and mouth.

Range: Only the northern Gulf of California, Mexico.

Why at risk: Entanglement in illegal gillnets set for totoaba fish.

16 · Ocean

Endangered

Whale shark

Rhincodon typus

Signature: The world’s largest fish carries a unique constellation of white spots.

Range: Warm oceans across the globe.

Why at risk: Targeted fishing, bycatch, vessel strikes, and marine pollution.

17 · Coasts & rivers

Critically endangered

Largetooth sawfish

Pristis pristis

Signature: A long, toothed rostrum works like a living metal detector and slashing blade.

Range: Tropical coasts, estuaries, and rivers.

Why at risk: Its saw tangles easily in fishing gear; nursery habitat is disappearing.

18 · Ocean

Endangered

Giant oceanic manta ray

Mobula birostris

Signature: Winglike fins span several metres, yet this giant feeds on tiny plankton.

Range: Tropical and temperate oceans worldwide.

Why at risk: Bycatch and targeted fishing for gill plates.

19 · Rivers to ocean

Critically endangered

European eel

Anguilla anguilla

Signature: It crosses the Atlantic to breed, while its transparent young return toward Europe.

Range: European and North African waters, migrating to the Sargasso Sea.

Why at risk: Dams, fishing, pollution, parasites, and changing ocean conditions.

20 · Rainforest

Endangered

Golden poison frog

Phyllobates terribilis

Signature: Brilliant yellow skin advertises one of the most powerful toxins found in an animal.

Range: A small area of humid rainforest in western Colombia.

Why at risk: A tiny range exposed to forest loss, pollution, and illegal collection.

21 · Asian forest

Critically endangered

Javan rhinoceros

Rhinoceros sondaicus

Signature: A single short horn and armour-like skin distinguish this rare forest rhino.

Range: One population in Ujung Kulon National Park, Indonesia.

Why at risk: Its tiny range leaves it vulnerable to disease, disasters, and habitat pressure.

22 · Asian forest

Critically endangered

Sumatran rhinoceros

Dicerorhinus sumatrensis

Signature: The smallest living rhino is covered in reddish-brown hair.

Range: Isolated forests on Sumatra and Borneo.

Why at risk: Forest fragmentation leaves too few animals close enough to breed.

23 · African savanna

Critically endangered

Black rhinoceros

Diceros bicornis

Signature: A hooked upper lip plucks leaves from thorny shrubs.

Range: Scattered populations in eastern and southern Africa.

Why at risk: Poaching for horn remains the greatest threat.

24 · African forest

Critically endangered

African forest elephant

Loxodonta cyclotis

Signature: Rounded ears and straighter tusks help it move through dense rainforest.

Range: Tropical forests of Central and West Africa.

Why at risk: Ivory poaching and forest loss have sharply reduced its numbers.

25 · Asian woodland

Endangered

Asian elephant

Elephas maximus

Signature: Smaller ears and a domed forehead distinguish it from African elephants.

Range: Fragmented habitats across South and Southeast Asia.

Why at risk: Habitat loss, conflict with people, and poaching isolate herds.

26 · Sumatran forest

Critically endangered

Sumatran orangutan

Pongo abelii

Signature: Long pale facial hair frames a highly intelligent great ape.

Range: Northern Sumatra, Indonesia.

Why at risk: Roads, farms, and plantations divide its remaining rainforest.

27 · Sumatran forest

Critically endangered

Tapanuli orangutan

Pongo tapanuliensis

Signature: Frizzier hair and a distinctive long call mark the newest recognised great ape.

Range: A small upland forest in northern Sumatra.

Why at risk: Development and fragmentation threaten its single population.

28 · Bornean forest

Critically endangered

Bornean orangutan

Pongo pygmaeus

Signature: Adult males develop broad cheek pads that amplify long calls.

Range: Forests of Borneo.

Why at risk: Logging, fire, plantations, and hunting continue to reduce populations.

29 · African forest

Critically endangered

Cross River gorilla

Gorilla gorilla diehli

Signature: Subtle skull and tooth differences separate this elusive gorilla.

Range: Forested hills along the Nigeria–Cameroon border.

Why at risk: Very small, separated groups face hunting and habitat loss.

30 · Congo forest

Endangered

Bonobo

Pan paniscus

Signature: Long legs, dark lips, and peaceful social bonds distinguish this great ape.

Range: Forests south of the Congo River.

Why at risk: Hunting and forest disturbance continue despite legal protection.

31 · Himalayan forest

Endangered

Red panda

Ailurus fulgens

Signature: A ringed tail and false thumb help it balance and grip bamboo.

Range: Temperate forests of the eastern Himalayas and southwest China.

Why at risk: Forest fragmentation, livestock, and poaching shrink its habitat.

32 · African highlands

Endangered

Ethiopian wolf

Canis simensis

Signature: A slender red-coated hunter specialises in catching highland rodents.

Range: Isolated alpine grasslands of Ethiopia.

Why at risk: Disease from domestic dogs and shrinking highland habitat threaten packs.

33 · African savanna

Endangered

African wild dog

Lycaon pictus

Signature: Every animal carries a unique patchwork coat and oversized round ears.

Range: Fragmented savannas and woodlands of sub-Saharan Africa.

Why at risk: Habitat fragmentation, conflict, snares, and disease break up packs.

34 · Asian forest

Endangered

Tiger

Panthera tigris

Signature: Each tiger has an individual pattern of dark vertical stripes.

Range: Scattered habitats across Asia.

Why at risk: Poaching, prey loss, and fragmented habitat isolate populations.

35 · European scrub

Vulnerable

Iberian lynx

Lynx pardinus

Signature: A spotted coat, beard-like ruff, and black ear tufts frame its face.

Range: Recovered but separated populations in Spain and Portugal.

Why at risk: Rabbit declines, roads, and fragmented habitat remain threats.

36 · Mediterranean coast

Vulnerable

Mediterranean monk seal

Monachus monachus

Signature: This shy seal often shelters and gives birth inside sea caves.

Range: Scattered Mediterranean and eastern Atlantic coasts.

Why at risk: Entanglement, disturbance, and loss of quiet coastal habitat persist.

37 · Pacific islands

Endangered

Hawaiian monk seal

Neomonachus schauinslandi

Signature: Loose neck folds give this tropical seal a monk-like appearance.

Range: The Hawaiian Archipelago.

Why at risk: Entanglement, disease, food scarcity, and rising seas threaten colonies.

38 · North Atlantic

Critically endangered

North Atlantic right whale

Eubalaena glacialis

Signature: Rough white callosities create a unique pattern on each whale’s head.

Range: Atlantic waters off eastern North America.

Why at risk: Fishing-gear entanglement and vessel strikes drive ongoing losses.

39 · Global ocean

Endangered

Blue whale

Balaenoptera musculus

Signature: The largest animal ever known feeds almost entirely on tiny krill.

Range: Oceans worldwide.

Why at risk: Ship strikes, underwater noise, entanglement, and changing prey remain concerns.

40 · Antarctic ocean

Critically endangered

Antarctic blue whale

Balaenoptera musculus intermedia

Signature: This immense blue whale follows seasonal krill blooms around Antarctica.

Range: Southern Ocean and Antarctic feeding grounds.

Why at risk: Historic whaling caused a collapse from which recovery remains slow.

41 · Tropical coasts

Vulnerable

Dugong

Dugong dugon

Signature: A downturned snout grazes seagrass like an underwater lawn mower.

Range: Warm coastal waters from East Africa to the western Pacific.

Why at risk: Seagrass loss, nets, hunting, and boat strikes reduce populations.

42 · Amazon rivers

Endangered

Amazon river dolphin

Inia geoffrensis

Signature: Adults can blush pink, and flexible necks help them weave through flooded forests.

Range: Amazon and Orinoco river systems.

Why at risk: Dams, nets, pollution, and deliberate killing threaten populations.

43 · Indus River

Endangered

Indus river dolphin

Platanista minor

Signature: Nearly blind, it navigates muddy rivers through precise echolocation.

Range: Fragmented reaches of the Indus River in Pakistan and India.

Why at risk: Dams, water extraction, pollution, and net entanglement divide its range.

44 · Arctic coast

Vulnerable

Polar bear

Ursus maritimus

Signature: Broad paws spread weight on ice and propel the bear through water.

Range: Sea-ice regions around the Arctic Ocean.

Why at risk: Rapid sea-ice loss shortens the time available to hunt seals.

45 · Chinese forest

Vulnerable

Giant panda

Ailuropoda melanoleuca

Signature: A modified wrist bone acts like a thumb for gripping bamboo.

Range: Mountain forests of central China.

Why at risk: Roads and development continue to fragment recovering populations.

46 · Australian woodland

Koala

Phascolarctos cinereus

Signature: Specialised teeth and digestion unlock a diet dominated by eucalyptus leaves.

Range: Eastern and southeastern Australia.

Why at risk: Clearing, drought, fire, disease, and roads reduce habitat.

47 · Tasmania

Endangered

Tasmanian devil

Sarcophilus harrisii

Signature: Powerful jaws and startling screeches belong to the largest living carnivorous marsupial.

Range: Tasmania, Australia.

Why at risk: A contagious facial tumour disease has devastated wild populations.

48 · Australian grassland

Critically endangered

Northern hairy-nosed wombat

Lasiorhinus krefftii

Signature: A broad muzzle fringed with whiskers helps this burrower forage at night.

Range: Two protected sites in Queensland, Australia.

49 · Madagascar

Endangered

Lemur catta

Grus americana
